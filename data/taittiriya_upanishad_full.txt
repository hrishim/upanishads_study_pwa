INTRODUCTION
The Taittiriya Upanishad belongs to the Krishna Yajurveda and is one of the ten principal Upanishads that form the core of Advaita Vedanta. It is divided into three sections called Vallis: the Shiksha Valli, which deals with phonetics and right conduct; the Brahmananda Valli, which introduces the famous teaching of the five sheaths (pancha kosha); and the Bhrigu Valli, which tells the story of Bhrigu's gradual discovery of Brahman through successive stages of understanding.

The pancha kosha teaching is one of the most influential frameworks in all of Vedanta. It describes the human being as consisting of five overlapping layers — the physical body (annamaya kosha), the energy body (pranamaya kosha), the mental body (manomaya kosha), the intellectual body (vijnanamaya kosha), and the bliss body (anandamaya kosha). The Upanishad invites the seeker to look past each of these layers in turn, asking whether any of them is truly the Self. The answer, arrived at gradually, is that the Self is what underlies and illumines all five — pure, unqualified consciousness.

The Taittiriya Upanishad also contains one of the most celebrated definitions of Brahman: "Satyam Jnanam Anantam Brahma" — Brahman is Truth, Knowledge, Infinity. This single phrase has generated centuries of commentary and remains a touchstone for Vedantic inquiry. The Bhrigu Valli adds a narrative dimension, showing through the teacher-student relationship how understanding deepens progressively when guided by direct experience rather than mere information.

Om. May He protect us both. May He help us both to enjoy the fruits of scriptural study. May we both exert together to find the true meaning of the sacred text. May our studies be fruitful. May we never quarrel with each other.
Om. Peace! Peace! Peace!
